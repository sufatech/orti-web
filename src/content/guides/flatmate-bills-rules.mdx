Sharing a home is more than rent: electricity, internet, shared groceries, sometimes a broken glass. When the tab is clear from the start, it is easier to talk about. This is not financial advice, just four suggestions for a shared household.

## 1. Who pays the bill, and where is it written down?

Bills usually come in one person's name. That person pays and the others pay their share later. What matters is that the payment is written somewhere; the "I think I already paid" conversation starts when there is no record.

## 2. Is the share equal?

If three people live in rooms of the same size, splitting equally is the simplest. If the rooms differ, or someone is rarely home, a percentage or an exact amount may be fairer. Whichever you choose, say the rule up front.

## 3. What happens when someone is away?

If one person is away for a month, does their share of electricity, water or internet change? That is for you to decide, but the tensest conversations come from the cases nobody decided. Record the month with an adjusted share.

## 4. How often do you settle up?

End of the month, every three months, or when someone moves out. Settling often keeps small amounts from growing. When you do, pay from your bank or in person and then record it, so nobody is left wondering whether it was closed.
